# One adaptable brand-guide design system

Use this reference when interpreting the brand inputs and Design On Acid ACID Method output or adapting the generator. There is one style; variation comes from the brand and analysis rather than a preset menu.

## From evidence to layout

Start with the actual logo geometry, palette, typography, imagery, audience, and use cases. Read the ACID Method output as a connected account of the brand. Its four dimensions work together:

- **Abstraction:** use the core idea, conceptual relationships, and degree of complexity to choose hierarchy, navigation, and visual explanation.
- **Creativity:** use the brand's approach to expression and experimentation to judge where scale, contrast, asymmetry, or visual surprise serves the identity.
- **Intention:** use purpose, practical constraints, and coherence to determine the grid, repetition, specification detail, and consistency of application.
- **Depth:** use emotional meaning, audience connection, and experiential qualities to shape pacing, whitespace, imagery, and the relationship between message and image.

Record the relevant findings and their design implications. Avoid automatic dimension-to-decoration mappings, invented scores, or replacing an existing method output with a generic personality label. Resolve contradictions through the intended audience and use cases; explain material choices.

## Construction and creative expression

These are qualities to balance within the same design system, not two styles or an automatic switch.

Where the evidence calls for construction, favor stable alignment, repeated modules, readable specifications, measured spacing, and clear hierarchy. Where it calls for creative expression, use deliberate changes in scale, controlled asymmetry, varied pacing, and brand-specific visual compositions. Both may coexist: expressive covers and applications can surround rigorously structured technical pages.

Typography, palette, corner treatment, imagery, and motifs come from the brand. Do not add generic rings, uppercase headings, patterns, or oversized section numbers merely to make a guide look creative. Do not impose the Design On Acid visual identity on another brand; the method guides reasoning.

## Content and composition

The available sections cover overview, logo, color and contrast, typography, visual elements, stationery, imagery, and applications. Choose, combine, or reorder them to suit the brand and supplied material. Use fixed 48 px outer margins on every side of each 1920 × 1080 slide. Align primary headings and cover logos to that left edge. Backgrounds may bleed.

Choose the page count from coverage and reader tasks. A concise identity can need fewer pages; complex channel standards can need many more. Do not duplicate decorative pages to reach a target.

Preserve precise color values, legible hierarchy, intact logo geometry and proportions, and clear distinctions between established standards and newly proposed rules. Patterns and mockups are optional brand extensions, not required decoration. Ensure logos fit fully and are not clipped in covers, applications, or repeated motifs. Follow explicit user alignment choices.

## Generator use

The renderer has one active base layout. The optional `layout` object stores the reasoning and exposes content-column position, headline scale, corner radius, and row spacing. See `brand-json.md` for the schema. These are implementation controls rather than a complete design model. Adapt frame methods and shared CSS when the evidence requires a different composition; do not just change tokens and call it bespoke.

Validate with actual assets. Inspect the contact sheet plus full-size cover, logo variants, backgrounds, clearspace, palette, typography, and an application. Check font loading, logo containment, collisions, text overflow, and contrast. Rebuild after corrections.

## Composition and rhythm

Build from a shared grid, type scale, spacing system, navigation and caption language. Reuse these anchors while varying the relationship of explanation and proof: a diagram with notes, a measured specimen, a compact specification table, a comparison, a complete contextual application, or a captioned image study. These are page compositions within one style, not brand-guide presets.

Match density to the reader task. Keep technical labels close to what they measure. Let conceptual pages breathe; give long copy an intentional reading measure. Section openings can establish pacing without repetitive giant numbers or copied divider recipes. Use dividers, rules, color fields, and micro-labels only when they serve navigation and the brand character.

Distinguish presentation layout from the thing being documented: an application may use different margins, type sizes, and aspect ratios. Show a flat technical layout alongside contextual mockups when practical details would disappear in perspective. Use a few labeled construction guides based on real geometry rather than decorative grids.

Before release, compare the guide with its brief: the visual system should be recognizable from this brand's assets and reasoning. Do not reproduce an external document's signature arrangement, palette, wording, decorative motifs or page sequence. The goal is an independently authored manual that works as a system.
